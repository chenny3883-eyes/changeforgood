Professional services website tab structure: suggested tabs, content, and image guidance for each page

Tab 1 — Home

1Hero section

A bold, one-line headline stating exactly what you do and who you serve. A two-sentence supporting statement. One clear CTA button (e.g. "Book a free consultation").

Image: you, in a professional but approachable setting — natural light, clean background. Avoid stock photos here. Real face = instant trust.

2Problem statement

2–3 short lines describing the specific pain your clients feel before working with you. This creates instant recognition.

Icon-based illustration or none

3How you help

3 short boxes: each names one core benefit or transformation. Keep them outcome-focused, not process-focused.

Simple Tabler-style icons, no photos

4Social proof strip

Logos of companies/publications you've been featured in, or a single powerful quote from a past client. Placed high on the page to establish credibility fast.

Grayscale logos on a light strip — keeps it clean and authoritative

5Featured service + CTA

A short preview of your most popular service with one link to the Services page. Ends with a warm, low-friction CTA ("See how it works →").

CTA: "Book a free call"

Tab 2 — About

1Your story

3–4 short paragraphs. Start with why you do this work, not your credentials. Clients aged 35–65 especially connect with authenticity over accolades. End with your credentials.

Image: genuine candid photo — at your desk, in a session, or outdoors. Avoid stiff corporate headshots. Natural and warm.

2Credentials and training

List certifications, degrees, affiliations, and years of experience. Use a clean visual list format — not paragraphs. This section is especially important for the 45–65 age group.

Small badge icons or certification logos — official and clean

3Your values or approach

3 short values that describe how you work. This tells clients whether you're the right fit before they even contact you.

No photo needed — clean typography works best here

4Media and features

Optional: links or logos of podcasts, publications, or interviews you've appeared in. Adds third-party credibility.

Grayscale publication logos CTA: "Work with me"

Tab 3 — Services

1Services overview intro

A 2–3 sentence paragraph explaining who this page is for and what to expect. Helps visitors immediately self-select ("Is this for me?").

2Service cards

One card per service. Each card: service name, 2-line description, who it's for, and a CTA. Keep to 3–4 services max.

Icon per card — no photos needed

3Process / what to expect

3–4 step visual showing how working with you unfolds. Reduces fear of the unknown — critical for first-time buyers of professional services.

Numbered steps or simple timeline graphic — no photos

4FAQ (per service)

5–7 questions per service. "How long does it take?", "What's included?", "Do you work with [specific situation]?". Answers objections before they arise.

CTA: "Get started" or "Schedule a call"

Tab 4 — Results (testimonials and case studies)

1Client testimonials

4–6 quotes from real clients. Include their first name, role/industry, and outcome achieved. Longer, specific quotes outperform short ones for trust-building.

Small circular headshots of clients (with permission). If unavailable, use initials avatars in a consistent style.

2Case studies (2–3)

Brief "before and after" stories. Challenge → your approach → measurable result. 150–200 words each. These convert 45–65 year-old analytical buyers more than anything else.

Abstract background or none. Avoid stock "handshake" or "meeting" photos — they feel generic.

3By-the-numbers bar

3–4 stat highlights: years in practice, clients served, satisfaction rate, etc. Displayed as large numbers in a horizontal strip.

No images needed — clean stat typography on a light background band CTA: "See if I'm the right fit"

Tab 5 — Resources (blog / insights)

1Article cards

3–6 articles on topics your clients care about. Each card: title, 1-line summary, category tag, and reading time. Builds long-term SEO and positions you as an expert.

Simple cover images: muted, editorial-style photos. Avoid busy or stock imagery. Consistent aspect ratio (16:9 or 3:2).

2Free download or guide

One lead magnet (PDF guide, checklist, or workbook) offered in exchange for an email. This is your primary trust-building and list-building tool.

Flat mockup of the PDF cover — clean, minimal design with your branding CTA: "Download free guide"

3Email newsletter sign-up

One-line value pitch + email input field. "Get weekly insights on [topic] — no fluff." Keeps warm leads connected without requiring a purchase decision.

No image needed here — keep it simple

Tab 6 — Contact

1Booking / inquiry form

Name, email, what they need help with, preferred time. Keep to 4–5 fields max. Longer forms deter the 50–65 group. Link to a calendar tool (e.g. Calendly) as an alternative.

Optional: small warm photo of you at your desk — makes the "who will I be talking to?" question disappear

2What happens next

3 brief steps after they submit: "You'll hear from me within 24 hours", "We'll schedule a 20-min intro call", "I'll send a proposal". Reduces anxiety about the unknown.

3Trust signals

Privacy note ("Your information is never shared"), response time, and optionally a short final testimonial. These reduce hesitation right at the conversion moment.

Lock icon or small badge — clean and minimal

4Social links and location

LinkedIn (essential for professional services), email address, and city/country. No need for full address unless you have a physical office clients visit.

CTA: "Let's talk"
